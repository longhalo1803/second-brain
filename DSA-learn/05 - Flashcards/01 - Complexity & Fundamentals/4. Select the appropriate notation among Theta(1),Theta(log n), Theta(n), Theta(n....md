### Q: 📝 4. Select the appropriate notation among $\Theta(1),\Theta(\log n)$, $\Theta(n)$, $\Theta(n \log n)$, $\Theta(n^{2})$, $\Theta(n^{3})$, $\Theta(2^{n})$, $\Theta(n!)$ to indicate the number of times the instruction $x:=x+1$ is executed.

**(a)**
for $i=1$ to $2n$
$x:=x+1$

**(b)**
for $i=1$ to $2n$
for $j=1$ to $n$
$x:=x+1$

**(c)**
for $i=1$ to $2n$
for $j=1$ to $i$
for $k=1$ to $i$
$x:=x+1$

**(d)**
for $i=1$ to $2n$
for $j=1$ to $i$
for $k=1$ to $\textcolor{red}{j}$
$x:=x+1$

**(e)**
$i:=n$
while $i\ge1$
$x:=x+1$
$i:=i/2$

**(f)**
$j:=n$
while $j\ge1$
for $i=1$ to $j$
$x:=x+1$
$j:=j/2$ #card
?
**(a)**
for $i=1$ to $2n$
$x:=x+1$
**Solution.** The number of times the instruction $x:=x+1$ is executed is equal to the number of times the for loop is executed, which we can write as:

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{2n}1=2n\in\Theta(n),
$$

where we used the formula for calculating the sum of the first k integers. In this case $k=2n$.

**(b)**
for $i=1$ to $2n$
for $j=1$ to $n$
$x:=x+1$
Solution. Each time the inner loop is executed, the instruction is executed $\sum_{j=1}^{n}1=n$ times.The inner loop is executed at each iteration of the outer loop, that is $2n$ times.So, in total the instruction $x:=x+1$ is executed

$$
2n \cdot n\in\Theta(n^{2})
$$

times.

**(c)**
for $i=1$ to $2n$
for $j=1$ to $i$
for $k=1$ to $i$
$x:=x+1$
**Solution.** The innermost loop executes the instruction $x:=x+1$ once at each iteration, for a total of $\sum_{k=1}^{i}1=i$.The innermost loop is executed at each iteration of the second loop (the one with index $j$), which in turn is executed once at each iteration of the outermost loop.So, putting everything together, we have:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{2n}}_{\small\text{outer loop}} \;\underbrace{\sum_{j=1}^{i}}_{\small\text{second loop}} \;\underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^{i} 1}_{\small\text{inner loop}} &= \sum_{i=1}^{2n} \underbrace{\sum_{j=1}^{i} i}_{(*)} \\
&= \sum_{i=1}^{2n} i\underbrace{\sum_{j=1}^{i} 1}_{=i} \\
&= \sum_{i=1}^{2n} i^2 \\
&= \frac{2n(2n+1)(4n+1)}{6} \in \Theta(n^3)
\end{aligned}
$$

where, for the last step, we used the formula for calculating the _sum of the first *$k$* squares_, with $k=2n$, and for the summation ($\ast$) we observed that the terms of the summation are independent of the index of the summation.
**(d)**
for $i=1$ to $2n$
for $j=1$ to $i$
for $k=1$ to $j$
$x:=x+1$
**Solution.** Similarly to the previous exercise, we write:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i=1}^{2n}\sum_{j=1}^{i}\sum_{k=1}^{j}1 &=\sum_{i=1}^{2n}\sum_{j=1}^{i}j \\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{2n}\frac{i(i+1)}{2} \\
&=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2n}i^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{2n}i\right) \\
&=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{2n(2n+1)(4n+1)}{6}+\frac{2n(2n+1)}{2}\right)\in\Theta(n^{3}).
\end{aligned}
$$

**(e)**
$i:=n$
while $i\ge1$
$x:=x+1$
$i:=i/2$
**Solution.** The instruction is executed once at each iteration, and the number of iterations is $\Theta(\log n)$.At each iteration of the while loop, the value of $i$ is halved, having after $k$ iterations: $i = \frac{n}{2^k}$.The loop terminates when the value of $i$ falls below 1, roughly when: $\frac{n}{2^k} \approx 1$.Multiplying both sides by $2^k$: $n \approx 2^k$. To isolate $k$ (the number of iterations), we take the base-2 logarithm on both sides: $k \approx \log_2 n$.
**(f)**$j:=n$
while $j\ge1$
for $i=1$ to $j$
$x:=x+1$
$j:=j/2$
**Solution.** For a fixed $j$, the instruction is executed $j$ times at each iteration of the outer loop. The values that $j$ can take depend on the outer loop and are $n,n/2,n/4,n/8,\dots 2,1$.So the number we are looking for is:

$$
\sum_{i=0}^{\log n}\frac{n}{2^{i}}=n\sum_{i=0}^{\log n}\frac{1}{2^{i}}=n\cdot\frac{1-\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\log n+1}}{1-\frac{1}{2}}=n\cdot2\cdot\underbrace{\left(1-\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\log n+1}\right)}_{=\alpha}
$$

where we used the formula for the k-th partial sum of a geometric series, for $k=\log n$.We have

```text
{1}{2}≤=1-≤ft({1}{2})^{ n+1}≤1 for n≥1
```

thus $\alpha \in \Theta(1)$ and

$$
\sum_{i=0}^{\log n}\frac{n}{2^{i}}=n\cdot2\alpha\in\Theta(n).
$$
